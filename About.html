It started with one
overgrown garden.

In 2014, Maya Rosewick inherited her grandmother's neglected garden on the edge of town. Under the weeds were forty-year-old roses, still blooming out of stubbornness.

She began cutting bunches for neighbours, leaving them on doorsteps with a handwritten note. The notes turned into orders. The orders turned into a studio, a field, and a team of eleven who all still argue about the best way to condition a peony.

Today we grow most of what we sell, buy the rest from farms within 80 miles, and never sell a flower that's been in a cold store for more than two days.

How we work

Seasonal only

No tulips in October, no dahlias in March. What's in the studio is what's growing right now, so it lasts longer in your vase.

Loose and wild

We arrange the way flowers grow: uneven, airy, with a little movement. Nothing stiff, nothing wrapped in plastic.

Gentle on the earth

Peat-free soil, rainwater irrigation and compostable wrapping. Every bouquet comes with a card on how to dry the stems afterwards.
